What problem is this project occupying?

Formal theorem proving for a research field is not only a sequence of isolated proof-search tasks. A sustainable library must preserve source meaning, avoid duplicate foundations, expose formal dependencies, and retain mathematical mechanisms discovered across papers and textbooks.

formalize→verify→connect→reuse

Two contributions

  1. Harness: theorem-sized workers, reuse-first retrieval, semantic round-trip denoising, independent verification, bounded memory, and serialized stabilization.
  2. Sampling Theory Lean Library: five coordinated mathematical routes plus three graph views for proof digestion and cross-pollination.
Contribution I

A theorem-driven Harness for durable formalization

One worker, one mathematical delta

A generalist worker owns a bounded theorem-sized advance end-to-end. Source reading, retrieval, proof design, Lean debugging, counterexample search, and exposition are modes rather than rigid hand-off roles.

Encoder–decoder semantic denoising

The source statement is encoded into Lean, reconstructed without exposing the original text, and compared by semantic slots: objects, domains, quantifiers, assumptions, conclusions, normalization, and edge cases. Repairs are proposed separately; the pinned source is never silently overwritten.

Durable discovery and verification

Reusable lemmas, blockers, counterexamples, and conceptual mirrors survive worker termination. A proving worker cannot self-publish independent verification; shared truth is admitted through a single stabilization lane.

Compiled case study

Strong convexity gives a reusable first-order quadratic lower model

\\[f(y)\\ge f(x)+\\langle G(x),y-x\\rangle+\\frac m2\\|y-x\\|^2.\\]

The anonymous Lean fixture contains the corresponding shared theorem and its curve-wise dependency. This is a concrete compiled substrate for the Euclidean curvature-to-growth pattern only; it does not certify PL, LSI, Riemannian, Wasserstein or Bakry–Émery implications.

Open Lean source

Proof digestion and planned graph compression

For a learner, the graph exposes exactly which prerequisites feed a theorem. For an expert, it exposes whether a new paper contributes a leaf, bridge, shortcut, hub or reorganization relative to an existing formal baseline. Compression is allowed only if expandable assumptions, provenance and original dependencies remain recoverable. The research goal is not to rank papers automatically, but to make mathematical reuse and structural novelty inspectable.
